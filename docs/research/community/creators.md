# OSRS creator landscape

Researcher: agent r2 (RAI-6). Date: 2026-06-21.

WebSearch was intermittently unavailable during this research window and
the YouTube API was not authenticated; the following profiles are
synthesized from project-owner domain knowledge plus public-channel
descriptions where reachable. Treat each row as "what marketing copy
should sound like to land on this creator's audience," not a verbatim
audit. Next loop should pull live channel metadata via `claude-in-chrome`
or YouTube Data API once a key is added.

The five creators called out in the RAI-6 brief — **J1mmy**, **Soup**,
**Settled**, **Limpwurt**, **B0aty** — collectively reach the vast
majority of the daily-engaged OSRS audience. Hitting their aesthetic is
how we earn organic mentions.

---

## J1mmy

- **Channel shape.** Highly produced commentary / mini-doc style. Strong
  comedic voice, polished editing, frequent collabs.
- **What they complain about (inferred).** Quality-of-life gaps in stock
  client. Repetitive grind narrative. Loves "finally, someone made this
  reasonable" moments.
- **Tone match for our copy.** Self-aware, slightly dry, leans into
  absurdity ("yes you do need a 200-quest-point chain to fight a dragon").
- **Marketing leverage.** A "J1mmy hands the assistant the worst possible
  quest backlog, the AI walks him through it" sponsor read writes itself.

## Soup

- **Channel shape.** Iron-man focused, optimization-heavy, "most efficient
  path to X" guides. Audience runs spreadsheets.
- **What they complain about (inferred).** Cost-per-XP volatility. Wiki
  guide rot. Plugin fragmentation (a different overlay per boss).
- **Tone match for our copy.** Numbers-forward, EHB/EHP-literate, dry.
- **Marketing leverage.** Ship a "one assistant, every boss" demo and the
  Iron community will share it. Lean into token cost / margin math in a
  blog post Soup's audience would actually click.

## Settled

- **Channel shape.** Documentary-grade long-form ("World's strongest
  pure", "1 HP only Ironman", "Maxing without combat"). Cinematic.
  Lives at the edge of game systems.
- **What they complain about (inferred).** That the game's complexity is
  *the* draw, and oversimplifying it kills the storytelling. They will
  scrutinize a product that looks like a bot.
- **Tone match for our copy.** Reverent, slow, lets the game breathe.
- **Marketing leverage.** A "Settled challenges the AI to plan a
  sub-optimal-but-spectacular path" angle — *the AI as muse, not as
  automation*. This is also our cleanest defense against "isn't this
  botting?" — Settled-style content forcibly proves we don't play for you.

## Limpwurt

- **Channel shape.** Long-running guides, drop tables, and money-making
  content. Encyclopedic. The "first video that pops up when you search a
  method" creator.
- **What they complain about (inferred).** The wiki is great but search
  is poor; players prefer the *answer* over the page. That's literally
  the gap our wiki tool fills.
- **Tone match for our copy.** Informational, calm, instructive. No
  hype.
- **Marketing leverage.** Sponsor a "AI vs. Limpwurt guide" video where
  Limpwurt's audience watches the assistant answer the same questions
  Limpwurt normally voiceovers. Our pitch in their language: "the answer
  you'd get from Limp, but it knows your bank."

## B0aty

- **Channel shape.** Long-form livestreams, charisma-driven, classic
  variety OSRS (PvM, PvP, jokes). Massive audience. Will absolutely roast
  a bad product on stream.
- **What they complain about (inferred).** Chat-help dilution, the
  ten-thousandth wiki-lookup mid-stream, awkward UI moments.
- **Tone match for our copy.** Loud, warm, real-time-reactive.
- **Marketing leverage.** A stream integration where chat triggers the
  AI to overlay quest hints during the stream — turns chat into a
  call-and-response moment. High-variance but viral if it lands.

## Outreach principles

1. **Lead with sponsorships that don't require them to recommend.** A
   creator using the tool on stream is more persuasive than an ad-read.
2. **Never pitch the automation angle.** Lean into "in-game co-pilot",
   "the wiki that knows you", "tile-marker oracle". Creators will sniff
   out any wording that smells like botting and they will *publicly*
   refuse it.
3. **Time outreach to content moments.** Leagues 6, Sailing skill
   anniversary, big quest drops, the next raid release — those are the
   weeks the creator is over-subscribed to questions and the AI shines.
4. **Free unlimited tier for partner creators**, branded with the in-game
   account name they stream with. The marketing site's "online agents"
   counter ticks up whenever they boot up. Free.
5. **Provide a clip pack.** Five 30s vertical clips of the AI doing
   something the creator's audience can recognize: clue solve, gear pick,
   quest next-step, boss prayer call, bank tab build.
